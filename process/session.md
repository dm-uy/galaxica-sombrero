# Session entry and handoff

<!-- anchor: process.session.baton -->

## Recoverable session

- **SESSION-101** MUST: Read the project's entry/current records in its declared order, then relevant links. Establish the goal, active task, live grant, source revision and foreign/uncommitted state before dependent action.
- **SESSION-102** MUST: Establish one writer per affected artifact; preserve others' work and follow the project's collision/isolation rules. A dirty tree permits no ungranted reset or discard.
- **SESSION-103** MUST: Record important facts when found; close by retaining packet/result, reconciling affected route/task/decision pointers and rewriting HANDOFF. Its sections are owner blockers, present/previous state, next authorized action and relevant traps. History stays in records.
- **SESSION-104** MUST: Preserve unknown effects, pending authority and failures across restart/compaction. Record checked revision and uncommitted ownership under actual project storage/commit rules.
- **SESSION-105** SHOULD: Check that a fresh reader recovers goal, current task, next authorized action, owner-only decisions, latest result and deferred work. Missing authority is resolved before dependent action; unrelated authorized work can continue.

<!-- anchor: process.session.entry -->

## Enter or resume the declared layer

The arriving contributor's inputs are the owner's current request, binding project entry, actual current records and their relevant linked artifacts. The output is a checked position: purpose, active task, matching authority, artifact/revision identity, permitted write scope, unresolved effects and next eligible action. The entry/profile gives the layer and path; a runtime's current directory alone does not. Single-layer is a useful default for new work, while an established layered project keeps its declared direction/delivery boundaries.

- **SESSION-106** MUST: Before re-asking a possibly settled question, look for the applicable specification gate, binding decision, handoff owner block and earlier answers in the current session; preserve the answer's exact scope and authentic provenance.
- **SESSION-107** MUST: Before handoff audit important observations, results, decisions and external state stranded in chat, routing them to their existing owning records with evidence/access limits; record relevant errors without starting an additional learning or experiment process.
- **SESSION-108** MUST: Reuse evidence only with its actual input/artifact identity, applicability and limits; retire an expired current trap into dated history with its expiry proof and recheck condition rather than erasing it.

1. Read the binding entry and resolve its adopted conventions, layer paths, reading order and storage/collision rules. Read VISION, the scoped ROADMAP, HANDOFF and TASKS in the declared order, then relevant current checkpoint links. A layered delivery contributor also reads the authoritative direction/decision inputs and does not edit the direction owner's layer. A missing preferred filename triggers recovery/classification, not greenfield bootstrap.
2. Compare the owner's current request with the recorded purpose and active task. Current human steering controls within the applicable authority; record any material changed scope through its actual decision process. Recover unfinished work and pending decisions instead of restarting from the first checkpoint. Prior failed/held history, phase ordering and cumulative repair limits survive compaction and session changes.
3. Inspect actual working state, source revision and run artifacts. Classify tracked/untracked/ignored state where relevant by ownership; unknown changes belong to another writer until established otherwise. Do not stash, reset, discard, commit or relabel foreign work. Establish one writer for each affected artifact; isolate or wait according to project collision rules. Read-only investigation can continue without a clean-tree fiction. Any granted edits remain within their named homes.
4. Verify the recorded current result against its actual output and checks. State whether evidence binds committed content, saved uncommitted content or another identified snapshot. An engine's state file or handoff summary is a pointer, not proof. Missing artifacts, conflicting revisions or an unknown consequential effect require investigation before dependent action; neither success nor failure is inferred from a timeout.
5. Look up prior authority before asking again: the relevant specification's gate record, applicable decision records, HANDOFF's owner blockers and the same session's earlier answers. Match the exact action/scope and current inputs. Cite the settling record in the task/baton. A clear existing human approval counts where governing authority permits it; an ambiguous response, default option, another agent's message or fictional filled record cannot supply it. Ask only for the genuinely missing decision and continue independent authorized work.
6. Select the next eligible action under that grant and the task's dependencies. On actual approved start, reconcile the current task/route and rewrite the baton promptly with the work's purpose and pointer. Follow any same-commit activation rule of the adopted project profile only when the commit is granted and safe. Do not wait until the work is finished to make its start visible.

The entry result names genuine blockers and the permitted continuation. A plain status question follows [read-only status](lifecycle.md) and edits nothing. Optional runtime goals or private memory do not replace the files, grant a restart or create a new goal without its required human request.

## Record facts and reuse proof honestly

Record a material fact when discovered, with what was observed, its task/context, actual artifact/input identity and evidence pointer. Put intent in VISION or the relevant approved requirement; a local choice/result/error in TASKS or its notes; a future-binding choice in the project's decision home; current blockers and hazards in HANDOFF. Elsewhere keep navigation pointers. This preserves one editable truth rather than repeating result bodies across current documents.

For an irreversible or otherwise consequential authorized action, preserve intent before the act under the actual storage policy: what, where, why and how the effect will be checked. When the action threatens its own evidence, preserve a recoverable copy outside the threatened scope in an authorized location. A timeout leaves the effect unknown. On resume inspect the target effect and retained intent before replay, seeking owner input where the grant or evidence cannot settle it. Intent storage does not grant the action itself.

Before reporting an earlier check as applicable, compare the inputs and checked artifacts with the current ones. Record the original command/observation, revision or content identity, time, outcome and limits. Reuse unchanged applicable evidence explicitly; say “reused” rather than implying a fresh run. A committed-state property needs proof at the relevant committed state even if an earlier working-tree check passed. Changed bytes, input revisions, configuration/environment or a failed/unavailable criterion can require new checks under the chosen method. Do not repeat a full suite solely to generate a new timestamp, nor waive a required check because another check passed. A mock/captured call proves only the observed call, not delivery at a real destination.

Treat generated figures and indexes as derived outputs of their actual sources. Compare any copied count or claim with that source before publishing it as fact; label estimates and unknowns. An index points to evidence and does not independently establish readiness. Record inaccessible or sensitive evidence through authorized pointers and explicit access limits; do not copy private payloads into current records.

## Close with a useful baton

The session custodian closes within its granted paths and the adopted storage policy. The output is retained packet/results, reconciled current records, a rewritten present baton and an owner-first closing message. History stays in its existing home; HANDOFF is rewritten rather than appended as a newest-first chronology.

1. Compare each task state and finish claim with actual artifacts/evidence. Retain the original packet/result before replacing current work; keep blocked, deferred, failed and unfinished rows with their next dispositions. Reconcile the route's achieved outcomes, focus and diagram together. Link decisions from the route instead of restating them.
2. Audit the session's own record for important facts no durable record carries: a decision only in chat, an outside file or ignored resource needed for the next action, a changed environment/configuration value, a defect or incident, a stale copied number, an evaluated alternative or unrecorded uncertainty. Route each finding to its single existing owner with task context/evidence. Preserve sensitive access limits rather than payloads. When deliberately omitting material from public/current records, record the authorized private pointer or reason and the practical limit. This is error/fact recording, not candidate ingestion, experiments, comparative metrics or a new learning workflow. Existing task repair/QA duties remain applicable.
3. Review each live trap: cause, relevant action, workaround, return/recheck condition and evidence. When its condition expires, a check now covers it or it becomes irrelevant under the adopted retention policy, move the record to dated history with the reason/proof and when to recheck. Remove only its current warning, not its history. A source-specific “three checkpoints” expiry applies only if the project adopted it. An expired trap cannot silently remain a permanent blocker.
4. Rewrite HANDOFF with owner blockers first, then present/relevant previous state, next authorized action and actionable traps. The owner block names every actual pending decision, the concrete action needed and its result pointer, or says “nothing.” State identifies the latest checked artifact/revision, limits and owned uncommitted state; Queue points to TASKS and its next task rather than copying the packet. In a layered project each custodian closes its own baton; requests for the other layer are explicit.
5. Run the applicable existing consistency/check procedures where required and authorized. Fix within-scope problems; retain unexplained or unavailable checks with their criterion and recovery action. Storage follows the project's actual rule: commit named owned files when granted and safe; otherwise report saved but uncommitted files and why. A foreign dirty path is reported and preserved, never bundled into the session's commit. If a commit changes a property tested only on committed content, check that property at the new identity. Retain earlier failed evidence and limits.
6. Read the baton as a new contributor: can it recover goal, task, next granted action, owner-only choice, latest result/evidence and deferred work without chat? Correct missing pointers under the grant. The final message leads with what the owner must decide, then concrete changes/checked result and confirmed findings/limits, including saved-state status. Do not report merge, publication or operational adoption that did not occur.

## Worked recovery and boundaries

The Meadow document review session below is fictional. Its consent, artifacts and checks illustrate recovery and provide no actual authority. The [HANDOFF](../documents/templates/HANDOFF.md) and [AGENTS](../documents/templates/AGENTS.md) forms supply complete editable and separately fictional filled records.

An imagined delivery contributor reads Meadow's entry: single-layer, current records at the declared root `.`, source text read-only, local document-review method. The handoff names B-002/T-202, P2 active and T-203 blocked for the missing appendix. The contributor follows T-202's gate pointer: the imagined earlier owner granted local comparison and drafting, with sharing reserved. That matching grant settles whether local drafting can continue; no repeated approval request is needed. A source inventory and comparison receipt bind source revision 1 and draft revision 2. Unchanged available-text comparisons reuse that receipt with its identity and appendix limit; newly supplied appendix claims require their own review.

The session observes an ignored reference folder required to reproduce a comparison and an earlier chat-only correction of a definition. It records the folder's owner/access pointer in the task notes and the correction with its source citation, then updates the draft's affected claim. It does not expose private content or assume the ignored folder is run-owned cleanup. A previous missing-link trap now has a verified replacement link; the custodian moves it to dated notes with expiry proof and recheck condition “source location changes again.” The current handoff retains the live missing-appendix trap only.

At close, the fictional baton leads with the owner action “supply the appendix or decide the review boundary; external sharing still awaits its own approval.” State identifies the actual draft/check identity and saved uncommitted local records under the declared policy. Queue points to T-202's remaining independent comparison and T-203's return condition. T-204 remains held for interviews; the earlier B-001/T-101 failed review remains in its retained packet, while the original blocked rows stay in B-002. A cold reader can continue permitted local work and identify both owner decisions.

If the only “approval” found is the fictional template, the next dependent action remains blocked. If an earlier send timed out, the session retains intent and investigates the destination before retrying; unknown is not failure. If a contributor reports “review ready” while the necessary source is inaccessible, the baton instead records readiness unproven and its missing proof. If closure encounters foreign edits, it preserves and reports them while saving only its own granted records. A status-only request reports these observed facts without rewriting any file. These boundaries preserve recovery without imposing clean-tree resets, automatic foreign commits, whole-history reads or a new runtime engine.
